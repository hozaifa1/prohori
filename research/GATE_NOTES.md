# PROHORI — Gate notes: every method choice, the paper behind it, how we use it

*Written 2026-10-19 with the gate work (TickTick D06 + D16). Paper IDs (A#, S#, M#) refer to [LITERATURE.md](LITERATURE.md); findings are quoted from the full-text reads recorded there. Papers marked **[foundational]** pre-date 2024: LITERATURE's inclusion rule keeps them out of the evidence base, so here they are cited only as the origin of a method, never to support a number. Numbers produced by this work are in `eval/results/gate_report.md` and `eval/results/panel_photos_2026-10-19.md`; this file says why each choice was made.*

## 1. Papers from LITERATURE.md and what they decide

| Citation | Finding | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A3** Angelopoulos et al., *Conformal Risk Control*, ICLR 2024 | For a loss `L_i(λ)` that is non-increasing in λ and bounded by B, `λ̂ = inf{λ : n/(n+1)·R̂(λ) + B/(n+1) ≤ α}` gives `E[L_{n+1}(λ̂)] ≤ α` for exchangeable data (Thm 1). | The certification gate threshold θ = −λ̂ is set by exactly this rule (`gate.crc_threshold`). Loss = fraction of a capture's measured cracks that are certified **and** wrong; the denominator m_i is fixed per capture, so the loss stays monotone in λ and bounded by B = 1. |
| **A4** Mossina et al., *Conformal Semantic Image Segmentation*, CVPRW 2024 | Post-hoc CRC on a model's outputs, no retraining, gives valid empirical risk. | Precedent for putting CRC *after* the measurement pipeline instead of retraining it: our estimator is classical OpenCV, the gate is fitted on its outputs. |
| **A5** Mossina & Friedrich, *Conformal Prediction for Image Segmentation Using Morphological Prediction Sets*, MICCAI 2025 | The nonconformity score is the smallest dilation radius λ with `Ŷ ⊕ B_λ ⊇ Y`; the margin is the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th smallest calibration score; it needs only the predicted mask. | In the width domain a symmetric dilate/erode by r changes the width by ±2r, so the score reduces to `|ŵ − w| / 2` per edge. We calibrate the width residual directly (`interval_mm`) and report the equivalent per-edge margin in mm and px. **Not implemented at mask level** (we do not publish nested dilated/eroded masks); the reduction is exact only for a uniform-width, boundary-offset error model. |
| **S8** Deng et al., CACAIE 2026 | A segmentation QA gate rejects a refined mask if its EDT maximum exceeds 2× the base mask's, or if it contains holes (contour hierarchy), and falls back. | `measure._measure_one` computes `edt_ratio` (max / median of the distance transform along the skeleton: we have one mask, not a base/refined pair, so the median along the skeleton replaces `d_max^base`), `n_holes` (contour hierarchy, speckle < 0.02 mm² ignored) and our addition `frag_frac` / `path_cover` (fragmentation). Reason codes `mask_blowup`, `mask_holes`, `mask_fragmented`; the 2.0 constant is the paper's, not tuned. |
| **M3 / S8** same paper | Even LiDAR measurement has 15–34 % relative error below 0.5 mm. | Motivates an *absolute* tolerance floor in "wrong": max(0.05 mm, 10 % of width), and reporting the interval per width bin. |
| **M2** Hassouna et al., *Sci. Rep.* 2026 | Phone + checkerboard, ≈10 px/mm: < 0.5 mm cracks read 11 % low with 22 % COV; reference instrument ±0.05 mm; mean relative error 12.65 %. | 0.05 mm (the reference resolution, = METRICS τ) is the absolute part of the tolerance; 10 % is chosen tighter than M2's 12.65 % and S8's 14.4 % mean relative error so "certified" is a stricter claim than either paper's average. Also the reason the gate is expected to refuse most thin-crack captures at low px/mm. |
| **M1** Jakubowski & Tomczak, krkCMd, *Sci. Data* 2025 | Reference width has an operator uncertainty of 16.6 µm RMS; resolution sweeps trace error against px/mm. | Reminder that the nominal printed width is itself uncertain (toner spread not corrected here, METRICS §1a protocol not yet applied); errors below ~0.03 mm are not over-read. The degradation sweep (downscale → lower px/mm) is the panel analogue of M1's resolution sweep. |
| **A1** Wu et al., *Q-Bench*, ICLR 2024 | MLLMs are weakest at recognising distortions; ~8 % below a senior human even for GPT-4V. | The LLM never judges pixels. Every gate input is a deterministic number (corner count, Laplacian variance, edge-spread blur σ, px/mm, obliquity, glare and saturation fractions, noise σ, crack coverage), all computed in `measure.py` and listed in `observation` / `quality`. |
| **A2** Zhang et al., *MLLMs Know Where to Look*, ICLR 2025 | MLLM accuracy depends causally on the subject's relative size; thin cracks sit in the failure regime. | Same design rule: the VLM is not asked to size cracks or to say whether a capture is usable. |
| **dos Santos et al.**, *J. Civ. Struct. Health Monit.* 2025 (AB level in LITERATURE) | Names blur, noise, contrast change and camera position as the dominant aleatoric factors. | Picks the degradation families in `eval/gate_degrade.py`: blur (Gaussian + motion), noise, JPEG, glare, extra perspective tilt, lower resolution. |
| **A6** Gao et al., ICRA 2025 | Viewpoint choice as optimisation of an explicit quality objective. | The gate's predicted difficulty `risk_ratio` and the counterfactual reason codes are that explicit objective and its gradient directions for the re-shoot loop. |

## 2. Foundational methods used (pre-2024, method origin only)

| Citation | What it is | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **[foundational]** Vovk et al., Mondrian conformal prediction (2003) and Papadopoulos et al., inductive conformal regression with a normalised nonconformity score (2002/2011); Lei et al., *Distribution-free predictive inference for regression*, JASA 2018 | Split conformal intervals from a held-out residual quantile; dividing the residual by a difficulty estimate gives locally adaptive widths; calibrating per category gives category-conditional coverage. | `interval_mm`: score `|ŵ − w| / (tol(ŵ)·exp(pred))`, quantile per measured-width bin and certified/flagged state (`gate.conformal_quantile`, `eval/gate_lib.fit_intervals`). The normaliser is the same learned difficulty that drives the gate. |
| **[foundational]** Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani, *Predictive inference with the jackknife+*, Ann. Statist. 2021 (CV+) | Cross-fitting: calibrate on out-of-fold predictions so every calibration point has a prediction from a model that never saw its group. | The score model is cross-fitted by **source photo** (5 folds) before CRC and the conformal quantiles are computed; the deployed model is refitted on all groups. CV+ guarantees are weaker (1 − 2α) than split conformal's, so the held-out group experiments, not the theorem, are what we report. |
| **[foundational]** Geifman & El-Yaniv, *Selective classification for deep neural networks*, NeurIPS 2017 | Risk–coverage curves and AURC as the evaluation of a reject option. | `gate_risk_coverage.png`; AURC is used to compare score models (`gate_report.md` §3). |
| **[foundational]** Immerkaer, *Fast noise variance estimation*, CVIU 1996 | σ from the response of a 3×3 Laplacian-difference mask (response σ = 6 σ_noise). | `measure._noise_sigma` (mean |response| over flat board pixels away from edges; median fallback). |

## 3. Decisions that are ours (not from a paper) and why

* **Exchangeable unit = a capture** (one derived image with all its cracks), not a crack: cracks in one photo share blur, pose and light, so treating them as independent would overstate n. The loss is averaged over a capture's cracks.
* **Groups = source photos** (burst frames merged). Derived variants of one photo are never split across calibration and test. A second, harder check holds out time-contiguous blocks of photos (same panel, light and pose) — in `gate_report.md` §5.
* **Score model** = a monotone-constrained gradient-boosted tree ensemble (depth 3, 100 trees) predicting `log(|err| / tol)`, exported as plain arrays in `gate_params.json` (numpy-only at run time, constant outside the calibrated range so it cannot extrapolate). Monotone: making any badness feature worse can never lower the prediction (unit-tested). Compared on held-out groups (`gate_report.md` §3): the additive models (sign-constrained ridge, logistic) certify 16 % of cracks at P(wrong | certified) <= 5 %, the monotone trees 29 %, unconstrained trees 28 %. So the monotone constraint costs nothing and interactions (resolution x blur x width) carry real signal. Single-feature rankings (blur sigma, px/mm, Laplacian variance) certify 0-0.5 % at that risk. An out-of-range guard refuses captures whose features are worse than the 99.9th percentile of the calibration data.
* **Reason codes by counterfactual attribution**: the smallest set of feature groups that, reset to their typical certified value, would make the gate pass. This keeps the old codes (`too_few_corners`, `low_resolution`, `blurry`, `oblique`, `glare`) and adds `noisy`, `low_coverage`, `unstable_width`, the mask-QA codes and `low_confidence`.
* **`uncertainty_mm` now = half-width of the conformal 90 % interval** (it used to be a hand-built 1-σ). The old heuristic is kept as `uncertainty_model_mm`, and is the "before" in every coverage table.
* **Honest limits.** One printed panel, one phone, one session (29 photos, 22 with a usable board). The degradation mixture (`gate_degrade.DIST`) is a design choice: the guarantee is for captures exchangeable with it, not for walls. CRC at *group* level is infeasible with ~20 groups (slack 1/(n+1) ≈ 4–5 %); the capture-level guarantee is validated empirically on held-out groups instead. The reference width is the nominal printed width (toner spread uncorrected).

## 4. krkCMd evidence folded in (D15, `research/WIDTHBENCH_NOTES.md`)

| Source | Finding | How the gate uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** krkCMd (Jakubowski & Tomczak, Sci. Data 2025), measured by D15 | Resolution-added error of the FWHM estimator on textured real concrete, bin 0.30-0.50 mm, MAE 80 / 70 / 66 / 52 / 41 / 33 um at 8 / 10 / 12 / 15 / 20 / 25 px/mm; D15 recommends 20 px/mm for a 0.05 mm-MAE claim. The final blend is 2-3x worse (197 -> 92 um) because it is unstable on textured profiles. | `gate.u_res_mm(px/mm, width)`: the five width-bin rows of that curve (0-0.1 ... 0.5-0.9 mm), fitted as `a * ppm^-b` per bin so it extrapolates, interpolated over width (`eval/gate_fit.py` re-checks the table against `krkcmd_metrics.csv`). **It enters three places.** (1) As a model feature `log_ures = log(u_res / tol)`: the CRC-fitted gate decides how much it matters on the printed panel. (2) As a candidate interval scale (`heuristic_res`, compared in `gate_report.md` section 5). (3) For `analyze(panel_mode=False)` (unknown, textured substrate) it is added in quadrature to the predicted error and to the interval scale (`gate.predict_eff`, gate mode `crc_textured`), because the panel photos cannot reveal substrate texture. In that mode the CRC guarantee is **not** claimed; it is a conservative surrogate. |
| Same | The 20 px/mm number is not copied. | What the fitted gate requires is reported per width below and in `gate_report.md` section 7. |

**What the fitted gate ends up requiring (printed panel, default level alpha_cal = 0.05, searched over 3-30 px/mm).** Resolution alone decides nothing for cracks >= 0.75 mm: they pass anywhere in 3-30 px/mm when the rest of the capture is good (in the calibration set 43-58 % of >= 0.5 mm measurements are certified in every px/mm band, at P(wrong | certified) 6-9 %). The width-dependent part is at the thin end: 0.4 mm needs >= 9.0 px/mm, 0.5 mm needs >= 18.1 px/mm, 0.3 mm passes only in the best conditions, and at the strict level (alpha_cal = 0.01) 0.4 and 0.5 mm never pass. On the 22 original photos the held-out gate certified 5, 3 and 0 of 22 cracks at 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 mm (none wrong), against 15-19 of 22 at 0.75-3 mm. In textured mode the u_res term makes a 0.3 mm crack fail at 8 px/mm while a 2 mm crack passes at 8 px/mm (`tests/test_gate.py`). **Read with care:** the panel result is conditional on a uniform printed substrate; krkCMd says textured concrete costs 2-3x more error at the same px/mm, so the textured-mode behaviour is the one that applies to walls.

## 5. Width conventions (kept explicit on purpose)

* **Panel ground truth = full printed stroke width** (`data/print/test_panel_ground_truth.json`, nominal mm, toner spread not corrected). Everything in `gate_report.md` and `panel_photos_*.md` is against this convention.
* **krkCMd `MANwidth` = the operator's black-core width** (greatest brightness gradient on each side of the dark core, bevelled crack-mouth shading excluded; Sci. Data 2025 Methods). `measure.py`'s integral estimators measure the full shaded valley, so errors against `MANwidth` (313 um RMSE for the blend) mix a definition gap with estimator error. krkCMd is therefore used **only as resolution-added error** (estimator at px/mm vs the same estimator at native, same profiles), where the definition cancels, never as an accuracy number.
* The two conventions must not be mixed in one claim: a wall crack's "width" has to be defined (core vs full shaded valley) before a panel-calibrated interval is quoted for it.

## 6. Estimator changes for textured surfaces (D15 section 6), and what was not done

| D15 finding | Action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mask-stage 6 mm running-max background (`_bg_map`) over-reads on texture | **Fixed.** `_bg_map` takes a local median of the 8-bit-quantised linear image (`BG_MODE = "median"`; `"max"` restores the old behaviour). | 300 krkCMd profiles, 0.1-0.6 mm, through the unmodified `_measure_one` (via `widthbench.pipeline_width`) at 12 px/mm: raw MAE vs `MANwidth` 0.87 -> 0.29 mm; stability between 12 and 20 px/mm (mean absolute change of the width) 0.47 -> 0.14 mm. Panel, 22 photos / 168 cracks: MAE 0.1014 -> 0.1014 mm overall, 0.0355 -> 0.0367 mm on the sharp photos (+1 um, noise level). No parameter was tuned for this, so it is not an in-sample gain. |
| +-1.2 mm profile window integrates substrate texture | **Tried and rejected.** A window that follows the crack (`PROFILE_WIN = "tight"`: half width + 4 sigma + 0.5 mm). | krkCMd stability 0.14 -> 0.09 mm, but correlation with `MANwidth` fell (0.22 -> 0.11) and the panel got worse (sharp photos 0.0367 -> 0.0418 mm). Kept as an option, off. |
| Fixed 0.12-0.15 mm smoothing dominates cracks < 0.3 mm | **Not done; D18 (estimator autoresearch).** Scale the smoothing to the measured blur sigma and re-tune `INK_RATIO` / blend switch points out-of-sample, because the blend parameters were tuned with the current smoothing. | - |
| Blend unstable between resolutions on textured profiles | **Not done; D18.** Candidates: fall back to FWHM (stable, 33-80 um resolution-added error) when the blend weight flips between neighbouring stations, or use a continuous weight. | - |

The dataset in `data/gatecal/` and the fitted gate were produced **with** the median background (the change was made before the calibration run), so the shipped gate matches the shipped estimator.

## 7. Results (the numbers the report will quote; sources: `gate_report.md`, `panel_photos_2026-10-19.md`)

* **Data.** 21 source-photo groups (22 usable photos; the two burst frames are one group), 2,584 derived captures, 18,683 crack measurements, 51 % wrong by the definition below (the degradations are severe on purpose). Variant 0 of each photo is the photo itself.
* **Risk.** wrong = |error| > max(0.05 mm, 10 % of the printed width); the CRC loss is the per-capture mean of certified-and-wrong over its cracks; B = 1.
* **Gate, default level (alpha_cal = 0.05, meta-calibrated).** Over 100 random splits that hold out 6 source photos: held-out certified-but-wrong risk **4.4 %** (5th-95th percentile 1.4-8.6 %), 38.5 % of cracks certified, P(wrong | certified) 11.9 %. Plain CRC at alpha = 0.05 gave 5.5 % on an earlier 20-split run, which is why the level is meta-calibrated on grouped splits. **Strict level (alpha_cal = 0.01):** risk 0.9 %, P(wrong | certified) 3.8 %, 25.0 % certified.
* **Every source photo held out once.** Original photos: 53.0 % of cracks certified, P(wrong | certified) 7.9 %; all derived captures 38.3 % and 11.3 %.
* **Interval.** Learned-scale signed conformal interval, Mondrian by measured-width bin x certified/flagged, nominal 0.94, claimed 0.90. Held-out coverage per true width 88.3 / 96.3 / 93.8 / 94.1 / 87.2 / 90.7 / 87.3 / 92.0 % at 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 3.0 mm, 91.4 % overall. Before (old `uncertainty_mm` as a +-1.645 sigma band): 99.7 / 99.5 / 99.6 / 98.9 / 94.8 / 90.8 / 62.7 / 72.7 %, i.e. far too wide on thin cracks and 20-30 points too narrow at 2-3 mm. The 0.3, 1.0 and 2.0 mm bins stay a little under 90 % under group shift.
* **Estimator out-of-sample** (leave-one-photo-out re-tune of `ink_ratio`, `d0`, `q0`): MAE 0.101 -> 0.104 mm over all 168 cracks, 0.037 -> 0.037 mm on the sharp photos, 0.045 -> 0.048 mm on cracks the gate certifies. The in-sample grid optimum (ink_ratio 0.98, d0 4, q0 0.4) differs from the shipped 0.90 / 5 / 0.5 and was not adopted, since that would be another in-sample tune.
* **Not claimed.** A CRC guarantee on real walls or on textured surfaces; a group-level guarantee (with 21 groups the group-level CRC slack is 1/22 = 4.5 %, infeasible at 5 %); mask-level morphological sets (only the width-domain reduction of A5 is implemented).
